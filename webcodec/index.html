Royalty-free video, end to end

Playing a format the browser does not know takes two steps: decoding it, and re-encoding the result into something the browser does play. The decoders below are royalty-free — Theora and Dirac both are — but the usual encoder is not: H.264 is pooled, and libx264 is published as licence-required.

So the encoding is handed to the browser itself. mm_solver carries GPAC's WebCodecs filters as built-ins, and use-webcodec="enc" asks for the encoder only: the picture is decoded by the WebAssembly accessor named in with, then encoded to H.264 by the codec your browser already ships. Nothing patent-encumbered is downloaded.

The "enc" matters. A bare use-webcodec also registers the WebCodecs decoder, which puts itself forward for the input track, fails on a codec WebCodecs does not know — Theora, for one — and does not hand back to the accessor you asked for.

Ogg / Theora

waiting…

The Big Buck Bunny trailer in full, 33 seconds of 1080p Theora with its Vorbis sound. The picture is decoded by theora_1 and the sound by vorbis_1, both read through ogg_1; the browser encodes them to H.264 and AAC, and isobmff_1 puts them in an MP4:

<video is="universal-video_1" registry="https://registry.bevara.ddns.net" force src="trailer_1080p.ogg"
       using="mm_solver_1.0.6" with="ogg_1;theora_1;vorbis_1;isobmff_1"
       use-webcodec="enc" use-worker controls></video>

Nothing plays until the whole file has been re-encoded: the 28 MB trailer takes about a minute on a laptop, and the players below wait for it in turn.

WebM / VP8, two minutes

waiting…

A two-minute excerpt of Elephants Dream, in 540×360 VP8 with Vorbis sound. webmdmx_1 reads the WebM, libvpx_1 decodes the picture and vorbis_1 the sound; the browser encodes both, as above:

<video is="universal-video_1" registry="https://registry.bevara.ddns.net" force src="elephants-dream.webm"
       using="mm_solver_1.0.6" with="webmdmx_1;libvpx_1;vorbis_1;isobmff_1"
       use-webcodec="enc" use-worker controls></video>

Your browser plays this WebM on its own; force sends it through the WebAssembly decoders anyway, to show a long film going the whole way. It is the slowest of the three, about three minutes on a laptop for 8.5 MB. Elephants Dream © 2006 Blender Foundation / Netherlands Media Art Institute, CC BY 2.5.

What works, and what does not yet

This is where the WebCodecs encoder stands for the royalty-free video families. The Theora and VP8 rows were measured with mm_solver 1.0.6, the others with 1.0.2:

FormatDecoderThrough WebCodecs
Theoratheora_1 works, sound included (Vorbis to AAC)
Diraclibschro_1 works
VP8libvpx_1 works, sound included (Vorbis to AAC)
MPEG-1 / MPEG-2libmpeg2_1 refused: Failed to connect mpeg2vdec to wcenc: Bad Parameter
Motion JPEGlibjpeg_1 refused: Filter not found for the desired type

The two refusals are in the encoder, not in the decoders, and neither can be worked around from the page. wcenc requires WIDTH and HEIGHT on its input at connection time, which mpeg2vdec only knows after parsing a sequence header; and no pixel-format conversion resolves between libjpeg's output and what the encoder accepts.

Length is no longer a limit. With mm_solver 1.0.2 on the page's own thread, a long source starved the session while the asynchronous WebCodecs callbacks were in flight: GPAC reported No data received from fetch for 5000 ms with an idle session and ended early, and this same trailer stopped after 1 second of its 33. With 1.0.6 in a worker it comes through whole, and so does the 2-minute VP8 and Vorbis film above; the cost grows with the length, about 1.5 seconds of work for each second of video on a laptop.

The same thing, without touching the page

The browser extension applies this to any site: it watches for media the browser cannot decode and runs the same pipelines, with the same rule that only royalty-free accessors are used unless you say otherwise.

The sample files and formats have been selected because they are licence-free to use. All demos are downloaded in their entirety, please be patient.
