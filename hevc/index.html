Decode HEVC video (MP4) using the <video> tag

HEVC/H.265 offers roughly double the compression efficiency of H.264 but carries licensing costs that keep many browsers from supporting it natively. This demo demuxes the MP4 container with isobmff_1, decodes the HEVC stream with libde265_1 and re-encodes it to H.264 with libx264_1 so that the native <video> element can play it back.

The video is played progressively: it starts within a few seconds and plays while the rest is still being transcoded, instead of after the whole file. The picture and the sound are cut into MP4 segments fed to the element through Media Source Extensions as they are produced. The HE-AAC sound is decoded by libfaad_1 and re-encoded to Opus by libopusenc_1. Transcoding runs a little slower than real time on a laptop, so playback may pause briefly at the edge of what is ready. A browser that cannot do it falls back to decoding the whole file; the line under the player says which way it went.

The decoder is libde265 1.1.3. Earlier builds of libde265_1 used 1.1.1, which rejects this stream's picture parameter set: no frame came out, libx264_1 had nothing to encode and the page stayed black, while the test, which then only waited for the transcode to finish, still passed. All 4267 frames now come out, and the #libde265 test checks the size of what it gets back. If your browser decodes HEVC natively, the <video> tag may start playing the source directly - that is the browser's own decoder, not this pipeline.

Code snippet

<video is="universal-video_1" src="hevcds_720p30_2M.mp4" using="mm_solver_1.0.6" with="libde265_1;isobmff_1;libx264_1;libfaad_1;libopusenc_1" progressive controls> </video>

Without progressive, the whole file is transcoded first - about three and a half minutes - and then plays. with="libde265_1;isobmff_1;libx264_1" then gives the picture alone, without the sound.
